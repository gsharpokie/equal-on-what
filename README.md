# Equal on What?

An interactive look at how U.S. women and men fare across 30+ measures: earnings, health, education, safety, power and time. The point: whether there is gender inequality, and in which direction, depends on what you measure, how you weight it, and when you look.

**Live site:** https://gsharpokie.github.io/equal-on-what/

## What it does

- Each measure is a tug-of-war row. The knot starts at "even" and moves toward whichever group fares better.
- Students predict the overall result first, then reveal rows one at a time.
- A year switch compares around 1980, around 2000, and the latest data.
- Each panel opens into extra measures (for example, test scores and degree fields under education).
- A build-your-own index lets users weight measures and choose whether to count gaps in both directions or only one, as some published indexes do.

## Data

All figures come from U.S. federal statistical agencies and other primary sources (Census Bureau, BLS, NCHS/CDC, NCES, BJS, HUD, SAMHSA, CAWP, Fortune, College Board). Each measure lists its source in the page. Data compiled October 31, 2026.
